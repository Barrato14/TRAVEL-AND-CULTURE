Nikko National Park (日光国立公園)

Located just a few hours north of Tokyo, Nikko National Park is a masterpiece of both nature and human artistry. It is home to the UNESCO World Heritage-listed Toshogu Shrine, the final resting place of Tokugawa Ieyasu, the founder of the Tokugawa Shogunate. The shrine is famous for its incredibly ornate wood carvings, gold leaf details, and the iconic "Three Wise Monkeys."

Beyond its spiritual landmarks, Nikko offers some of Japan's most dramatic natural scenery. From the 97-meter-high Kegon Falls to the serene waters of Lake Chuzenji, the park is a paradise for hikers and photographers alike. In the autumn, the mountainside transforms into a sea of vibrant reds and golds, making it one of the most sought-after destinations for Koyo (autumn leaf) viewing in the entire country.
