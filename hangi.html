Hāngī

Passed down through generations, the Hāngī is a traditional New Zealand Māori method of cooking food using heated, volcanic rocks buried in an underground pit oven (or umu). It is much more than just a meal; it is a profound cultural experience centered around community and hospitality.

Meats like pork, chicken, and mutton, along with root vegetables such as kūmara, potato, and pumpkin, are carefully wrapped and placed in baskets to steam for several hours. The result is a feast with a tender, earthy, and smoky flavor: A shared meal that brings people together and connects them directly to the earth.
