Ogasawara Islands (小笠原諸島)

Often referred to as the "Galapagos of the Orient," the Ogasawara Islands are a remote volcanic archipelago located roughly 1,000 kilometers south of Tokyo. Accessible only by a 24-hour ferry ride, these islands have never been connected to a continent, leading to the evolution of a unique ecosystem where many species of flora and fauna are found nowhere else on Earth.

The islands are a sanctuary for nature lovers, offering world-class whale watching, dolphin swimming, and trekking through subtropical forests. Because of their isolation, the Ogasawara Islands maintain a distinct "island culture" that blends traditional Japanese hospitality with a deep-rooted respect for environmental conservation. Whether you are exploring the white sands of Minamijima or diving into the "Bonin Blue" waters, the islands offer a rare glimpse into a prehistoric, untouched world.
