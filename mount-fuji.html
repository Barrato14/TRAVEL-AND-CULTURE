Mount Fuji (富士山)

Rising majestically to 3,776 meters, Mount Fuji (Fujisan) is not just Japan’s highest peak—it is the very soul of the nation. An active stratovolcano with a near-perfect symmetrical cone, it has been worshipped as a sacred mountain for centuries. It was recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2013, not only for its geology but for its immense influence on Japanese art and spiritual life.

The mountain’s history is divided into three distinct geological phases: the ancient Komitake, the "Old Fuji," and the current "New Fuji" (Shin Fuji), which formed roughly 10,000 years ago. While it last erupted in 1707 during the Hoei era, it remains a "sleeping" giant that continues to inspire. Every summer, thousands of pilgrims and hikers ascend its slopes to witness the Goraiko—the breathtaking sunrise from the summit—a tradition that blends physical endurance with spiritual renewal.
