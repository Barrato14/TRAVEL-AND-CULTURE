Japan Culture

Explore some of the most known culture in Japan

⬅ Back to Journey

Manga

Manga, Japanese graphic narratives combining art and text, evolved from 13th-century scrolls into a global pop-culture phenomenon. Rooted in Edo-period ukiyo-e art, modern manga blossomed post-WWII under influences like Osamu Tezuka, blending American cartoons with cinematic storytelling. It is characterized by black-and-white art, vertical reading (right-to-left), and varied demographics.

Samurai

Samurai were Japan’s elite warrior class, emerging from provincial protectors in the Heian period (794–1185) to dominate Japan as military dictators, officials, and cultural leaders for nearly 700 years.

Kyoto

Kyoto is renowned for its historic temples, gardens, and natural beauty. Visitors can enjoy cherry blossoms, autumn leaves, and traditional Japanese landscapes.

Mount Fuji (Fujisan)

Mount Fuji is an active stratovolcano, 3,776 meters high, formed over hundreds of thousands of years through three distinct phases: Komitake, Ko Fuji ("Old Fuji"), and the current Shin Fuji ("New Fuji"). It last erupted in 1707 and has deep cultural significance as a sacred, artistic symbol in Japan.

Nikko National Park

Nikko National Park features mountains, lakes, waterfalls, and historic temples. It’s a perfect destination for hiking and experiencing Japan’s lush nature.

Ogasawara Islands

A remote island chain, the Ogasawara Islands are known for pristine beaches, clear waters, and rare wildlife, often called the “Galapagos of the Orient.”
